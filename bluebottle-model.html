Bluebottle stranding forecast

Cabbage Tree Bay, Manly: how the wind-drift risk band works, what it was fitted on, and where it falls short.

By Marco Bordieri
Bay Diver & Creator

An operational wind-drift risk band, shipped in Swim Manly, a public swim-forecast app for Cabbage Tree Bay. Wind-only, by design. It reports relative risk, never probability. The full derivation and its history are in the complete write-up on GitHub.

Model

Physalia are wind-drifted surface sailors, so arrival is governed by onshore wind: not swell, not SST. Per hour t:

onshore(t)
= mean over the 3 h up to t of max(0, cos(windFrom − 82°)) × speed
windPos
= piecewise-linear position of onshore on thresholds [4, 6, 12] km/h → bands Low / Moderate / High / Extreme, relative risk 0.4 / 1.3 / 2.3 / 3.3 ×
risk(t)
= windPos × seasonMult[month]
  • 82° is the bay’s ENE shoreline aspect. Offshore (cos < 0) clamps to 0 by design: an offshore wind does not retrieve animals already inshore.
  • 3 h lag from a sweep of [1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 12, 24, 48] h. Single-feature AUC peaks at 1–6 h (3 h nominally sharpest) and collapses beyond ~9 h, consistent with a recent-push mechanism.
  • Display grain is the day, rated by its worst 3 h window; the chart bins 4-hourly at the max, so short onshore peaks are never smoothed away.

Seasonality

Season enters multiplicatively as an abundance term, not as a cap:

Read it as likelihood = wind favourability × how many animals are offshore to be blown in. The same strong onshore wind reads Extreme in January and Low in July.

The original implementation clipped the wind band to a monthly ceiling, which put windy out-of-season days on a flat plateau. Multiplying gives one continuous likelihood and lets a windy July day ease down smoothly. Wind thresholds and relative risks were unchanged in that switch; only the wind–season combination changed.

Caveat worth your attention. The envelope’s shape is solid: it comes from monthly distinct-stranding-day counts and matches the ~50%-summer, near-zero-winter record in Bourg et al. (2022), so it is not an observation-effort artefact. The magnitudes, though, are an approximate envelope rather than a fit.

A related trap: the predecessor cap normalised monthly counts by the maximum, and October (107 distinct stranding-days) against January (68) meant one exceptional month depressed every other month’s factor. Mean- or median-normalisation behaves better.

Data and design

  • Sightings: iNaturalist, genus Physalia, taxon query over a Sydney bounding box (Cronulla to Palm Beach), not the curated project, which holds only ~400 hand-added records. 718 returned, 705 after dropping geoprivacy = obscured (fuzzed to ~20 km). 556 fall in the 2021–26 hourly wind span, giving 443 stranding-days after collapsing same-day, same-beach reports. Local subset: 48 records, 36 distinct days, 2011–2026.
  • Wind: Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis, cross-checked against the ~2–11 km Historical Forecast model. Identical skill: bluebottle drift is synoptic-scale, so wind-field resolution doesn’t change the answer.
  • Design: matched case–control, 3 controls per case drawn from typical days at the same beach cluster. This is the load-bearing choice: it controls for observation effort (where people look) and isolates the temporal wind signal. Presence-only data means the output is necessarily relative risk, not calibrated probability; absolute probability needs none-seen data.
  • Skill: AUC under year-blocked cross-validation, so temporally autocorrelated events can’t leak across folds.

Results

ModelAUC
Region, onshore wind alone0.82
Region, + per-beach aspect and lag sweep0.86
Cabbage Tree Bay (48 records)0.89
90% CI 0.82–0.95

Negative results, all tested and all no improvement: waves, sea temperature, higher-resolution wind fields, per-beach fetch weighting, and an explicit two-stage (E-then-N) drift term. A fetch-by-direction weighting actively lost to the plain 82° cosine (0.84 vs 0.89). The simple onshore band is the parsimonious best, and that is the most transferable finding here.

Band multipliers are each band’s stranding-day share over the overall share in the matched set. The set is 1 : 3.7 case:control, not the true base rate, hence “× a typical day” and never a percentage.

Limitations

  • Relative, not absolute: there is no “100%”, and even Extreme means elevated likelihood, not certainty.
  • The local sample is small (48 sightings); a wind-decoupled event could sit below detection.
  • Surface currents are unmodelled. The EAC and local eddies add non-linearity that wind alone can’t capture. This is a wind-drift model, not a transport model.
  • Rip currents, a known beach-scale predictor, can’t be fed in: no forecast product exists.
  • The shipped first-band threshold is 4 km/h, raised from an empirically placed 2 km/h because 2 km/h fired the warm band on near-calm days. That is display conservatism layered on the fit, not a re-estimation. Use 2 km/h if you’re reproducing the quartile placement (25th/50th/75th percentiles of prior-3 h onshore on stranding-days ≈ 1.4 / 7.8 / 13.4 km/h).
